1. Purpose and Nature of the Request for Information (RFI)
The Department of National Defence (DND) is requesting Industry feedback regarding the procurement 
of fourteen (14) new Diesel Driven Fire Pumps (DDFP) for use by the Royal Canadian Navy (RCN) on the 
Halifax-Class Frigates. The DND is also requesting an option to procure twelve (12) additional DDFP’s if 
necessary. 
The objective of this RFI is to share the DND’s requirement and seek feedback from Industry on potential 
options to meet the DND’s needs. This is the first step of a procurement process and is intended to 
determine availability of goods, Industry capability to meet our technical requirements, and to provide 
sustainment solutions. 
This RFI is neither a call for tender nor a Request for Proposal (RFP). No agreement or contract will be 
entered into based on this RFI. The issuance of this RFI is not to be considered in any way a commitment 
by the Government of Canada, nor as authority to potential respondents to undertake any work that 
could be charged to Canada. This RFI is not to be considered as a commitment to issue a subsequent 
solicitation or award contract(s) for the work described herein. 
Although the information collected may be provided as commercial-in-confidence (and, if identified as 
such, will be treated accordingly by Canada), Canada may use the information to assist in drafting 
performance specifications (which are subject to change) and for budgetary purposes. 
Respondents are encouraged to identify, in the information they share with Canada, any information 
that they feel is proprietary, third party or personal information. Please note that Canada may be 
obligated by law (e.g. in response to a request under the Access of Information and Privacy Act) to 
disclose proprietary or commercially-sensitive information concerning a respondent (for more 
information: http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/a-1/). 
Respondents are asked to identify if their response, or any part of their response, is subject to the 
Controlled Goods Regulations. 
Participation in this RFI is encouraged but is not mandatory. There will be no short-listing of potential 
suppliers for the purposes of undertaking any future work as a result of this RFI. Similarly, participation 
in this RFI is not a condition or prerequisite for the participation in any potential subsequent solicitation. 
Respondents will not be reimbursed for any cost incurred by participating in this RFI. 
The RFI closing date published herein is not the deadline for comments or input. Comments and input 
will be accepted any time up to the time when/if a follow-on solicitation is published.

2. Background Information 
 
 
 
The existing Diesel Driven Fire Pumps (DDFP) configuration consists of a Deutz Motoren Werke 
Mannheim (MWM )D226 diesel engine driving a FF704121 INGERSOLL-RAND centrifugal pump. These 
components are installed directly onto a steel base which is mounted onto the ship’s hull by means of 
four (4) resilient mounts. 
 
The Deutz MWM D226 engines are obsolete. 
 
 
3. Potential Work Scope 
 
The DND intends to procure fourteen (14) DDFP, plus Spares, Packaging, and Support &Test Equipment 
(S&TE) for recommended first and second level maintenance enough to ensure maintenance of the 
engines for two years, and to allocate work responsibilities between Canada and the Contractor. In 
addition, DND is requesting an option to procure an additional twelve (12) DDFP. 
 
The term “DDFP” includes the diesel engine and centrifugal pump solidly mounted onto a steel sub-base 
and connected to each other by a flexible coupling.  It also includes any appendages mounted off the raft 
assembly such as control panels, motor starters, and freshwater expansion/header tanks, etc., that are 
intended to be fitted in the ship separately. 
 
Installation of the DDFPs into the ships will be conducted by Canada either in a shipyard contracted by 
Canada during a contracted refit period or within one of His Majesty’s Canadian (HMC) Dockyards. 
 
 
 
4. Procurement Approach 
 
The current plan is to solicit a competitive Solicitation of Offers on Canadabuys for design, testing,

5. Technical 
 
 
5.1 Mandatory Technical Requirements 
 
1) Capacity 147 m3/h and total head 10 bar 
 
2) Weight no more than 1,214 kg complete without fluids 
 
3) Fit in the following dimensions: 
a) Length 80 inches  
b) Width 41 inches 
c) Height 52 inches including the mounts 
 
4) Duty Cycle: 100% load continuous load for 100 hours/year. 
 
5) Must maintain rated output under the following conditions: 
a) Roll: +/- 40° for a 10 second period (i.e. from 0° to starboard to 40° port and back to 0° 
within a 10 second period) 
b) Pitch: +/- 10° within a 6 second period 
c) Permanent list: +/- 20° max 
d) Permanent trim: +/- 5° max 
 
6) Must withstand and operate under the following ambient environments: 
a) Fresh Air Supply 
 
i) -32°C min (mandatory) 
ii) -40°C min (desirable) 
iii) +48°C max (mandatory) 
b) Sea Temperature: 
i)  -2.2°C min; 35°C max 
c) Relative Humidity 
 
 
i) 0% min; 100% max 
d) Machinery Space Temp  
i) -2°C min; 57°C max 
 
7) Fuel Type: standard Naval distillate fuel to Canadian General Standards Board CGSB-3.11-2017, 
with sulphur content of 15 ppm or less supplied by the existing Fuel Oil Services System 
 
8) Engine Lubrication: 15W40 DEO-ULS CAT Oil 
 
9) The DDFP to be installed will be lowered into the ships’ spaces through the hatches that are 
914mm x 914 mm large. The design must allow the possibility that the DDFP, including the base

15. Annex A – Requirement 
 
The Contractor must ensure that the items delivered to Canada are accompanied by Certification of 
conformance from the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) to meet Canada regulatory and 
assurance requirements. 
15.1 Integration Requirements 
The Contractor must design the DDFP into the Physical Envelope Requirements stated in section 5, and 
the Contractor must integrate the DDFP fully with the existing System Alarm, Monitoring, Control, and 
Safety Device signals. 
The Contractor must develop and deliver an Interface Control Document (ICD) to ensure that interface 
management processes are in place to identify and control all external and internal interfaces. The 
Contractor must identify each interface and describe its purpose in the ICD. 
The Contractor must include in the ICD installation specifications and directions which will form part of 
the Department of National Defence (DND) Engineering Change package. Canada may provide this 
specification to a third party involved in the installation. 
15.2 Manuals 
The Contractor must provide operating and maintenance manuals. The manuals must detail all 
maintenance activity and procedures for DDFP and component repairs, strip-down and overhaul. 
The Contractor must provide parts manuals. The manuals must provide a complete breakdown of all 
DDFP component parts. 
The contractor must provide a letter to provide the right to DND to share the manuals with a third party 
for the development of the preventive maintenance schedules and instructions. 
15.3 Provisioning Documentation (PD)
